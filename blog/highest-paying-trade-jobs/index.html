Highest-Paying Trade Jobs in 2026 (Ranked by Pay)

Updated October 2, 2026 · ~7 min read · By Skyra Roadmap

The skilled trades are having a moment — and for good reason. Strong pay, no tuition debt, and jobs that can't be shipped overseas. Here are the highest-paying trades in 2026, ranked.

Pay figures below are U.S. medians; the top quarter of earners in each trade — and anyone who runs their own business — makes considerably more. "Training" shows the typical path in, most of which you're paid to complete.

The ranking

Median ≈ $102,420/yr

1. Elevator Installer & Repairer

Training: 4-year paid apprenticeship

Install and service elevators and escalators. Consistently the highest-paid trade, with strong union pay and benefits.

Median ≈ $113,080/yr

2. Commercial Pilot

Training: Flight school + hours

Not a classic 'trade,' but no 4-year degree needed — flight training and logged hours. Cargo and charter demand is strong.

Median ≈ $85,420/yr

3. Power Line Installer

Training: On-the-job training

Build and repair the electrical grid. High pay for hazardous, skilled outdoor work; little schooling required upfront.

Median ≈ $104,900/yr

4. Construction Manager

Training: Experience + certificate

Run job sites and crews. Many rise from the trades without a degree, promoted on skill and reliability.

Median ≈ $98,300/yr

5. Radiation Therapist

Training: 2-year associate degree

Deliver cancer treatments. Among the best pay-to-schooling ratios of any health career.

Median ≈ $87,530/yr

6. Dental Hygienist

Training: 2-year associate + license

Clean teeth and coach patients. Flexible hours, strong pay, two years of training.

Median ≈ $61,590/yr

7. Electrician

Training: 4-5 yr paid apprenticeship

Wire buildings and systems. Earn while you learn; solar and EV work is driving demand up. Self-employment pushes pay far higher.

Median ≈ $61,550/yr

8. Plumber

Training: 4-5 yr paid apprenticeship

Install and repair piping. Recession-resistant and highly self-employable once licensed.

Median ≈ $75,020/yr

9. Aircraft Mechanic

Training: 18-24 month FAA program

Maintain and certify aircraft. FAA certification, steady airline and cargo demand.

Median ≈ $62,580/yr

10. Wind Turbine Technician

Training: 2-year certificate/degree

One of the fastest-growing jobs in the country. Climb and service turbines; green-energy tailwind.

Median ≈ $57,300/yr

11. HVAC Technician

Training: 6-24 month program

Install and service heating/cooling. Year-round demand in nearly every climate.

Median ≈ $53,620/yr

12. Welder (specialized)

Training: Months to 2 years

Underwater and pipeline welding pay far above the median; specialization is the lever here.

Why the trades are a smart bet right now

  • Retirement wave: experienced tradespeople are aging out faster than new ones are coming in, pushing wages and demand up.
  • No debt: apprenticeships pay you to learn — the opposite of a tuition bill.
  • Can't be offshored or automated away: someone has to physically wire the building and fix the furnace.
  • Business upside: licensed tradespeople can start their own companies and multiply their income.
Reality check: trades are physical and the early apprenticeship years aren't glamorous. They're a fantastic fit for the right person — and a grind for the wrong one. That's exactly why fit matters more than the salary number.

How to get started in a trade

The first step is almost always one of three things: a paid apprenticeship (search "[your state] + [trade] + apprenticeship"), a community-college or trade-school program, or an entry-level helper job that trains you up. Frequently asked questions

What is the highest-paying trade job?

Elevator installers and repairers are consistently the highest-paid skilled trade, with median pay around $102,000 and no college degree required — just a paid apprenticeship. Power-line installers, radiation therapists, and specialized welders also reach very high pay.

Which trades pay six figures?

Elevator installers, construction managers who rise from the trades, and specialized welders (underwater, pipeline) regularly clear $100,000. Electricians and plumbers who run their own businesses often pass six figures too, even though the median employee wage is lower.

How long does it take to train for a trade?

It varies a lot: some certificate programs take 6–24 months, health roles like dental hygiene take about two years, and apprenticeships (electrical, plumbing, elevator) run 3–5 years — but you're paid the whole time, so there's no tuition debt.

Are the trades a good career choice in 2026?

Yes. A wave of retirements is creating worker shortages, the work can't be offshored, and pay is rising. For hands-on people, a trade often beats a four-year degree on both pay and debt.
